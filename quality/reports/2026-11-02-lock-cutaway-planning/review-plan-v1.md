I propose a **22-second cutaway called “Why the right key turns.”** One lock stays on screen throughout: first blocked, then responding to the entering key, then visibly free to rotate.

The visual emphasis is the contact chain: **key surface → bottom pin → top pin and spring → aligned join.** After insertion, we pause so the viewer can inspect all five joins before the turn begins. Page one of the [manufacturer’s explanation](https://s7d5.scene7.com/is/content/BDHHI/reykey%20instruction/pin-tumber-locks.pdf) supplies the mechanical basis: varying bottom-pin lengths match the key’s cuts, bringing their tops to the plug boundary—the shear line.

The look is a dark charcoal stage, satin brass components, soft directional highlights and restrained cyan alignment marks. Motion feels controlled and physical, with no bounce in the mechanism. Short captions carry the explanation without narration.

**Proposed construction brief**

These are proposed defaults for approval:

- **Format:** 1920 × 1080, 16:9, 30 fps, 22 seconds. Landscape gives the five pin pairs enough space for clear contact and comparison.
- **Composition:** One shallow three-quarter cutaway, large in the center. Its visible front face establishes the cylinder’s axis; the lengthwise opening exposes the pin pairs. The camera stays fixed during contact, alignment and rotation.
- **Reading order:** Key contact, moving pins, aligned joins, turning cylinder.
- **Artwork:** Original, deliberately simplified vector geometry: housing, plug, key, five bottom pins, five top pins and five springs. Dimensions and key profile are illustrative. Brass uses broad tonal shading, fine edge highlights and small contact shadows.
- **Palette:** Charcoal `#10161C`, brass `#B58A46`, light brass `#E3C48A`, cyan `#69D5DF`, warm white `#F2EEE6`. Top and bottom pins have distinguishable brass values.
- **Type:** Proposed Arial Regular/Bold, subject to later availability verification; an available neutral sans is the fallback. Main copy approximately 52 px, labels 30 px. Copy occupies the upper band; labels sit outside the mechanism.
- **Audio:** Silent by default; the explanation is complete visually.
- **Ownership:** A future new project and comp, `PIN_TUMBLER_EXPLAINER_v01`, with editable shapes, text and controls. No existing project is assumed.

**Construction and review criteria for a later build**

The installed plugin’s construction, continuity, material and evidence guidance informs this plan. The proposed implementation uses editable vector surfaces projected from a shared cylinder coordinate system, with separate controls for key insertion and axial rotation. Pin contact, spring length and occluding surfaces share that geometry.

The difficult passage is alignment becoming rotation. All component identities persist across it; there is no replacement lock or hidden reset. Planned inspection frames include:

- **107/108/109:** first contact and the first driven response.
- **153, 198 and 243:** intermediate insertion, checking contact and spring behavior.
- **269/270/271:** arrival at the fully seated state.
- **299/300/301:** physical alignment preceding the explanatory highlight.
- **434/435/436 and 472:** onset and midpoint of rotation.
- **510 and 659:** resolved turn and final reading pose.

A later build should first prove one ordinary contact passage and the alignment-to-turn passage. Acceptance depends on visible contact, accurate separation at the shear boundary, coherent axial rotation and readable copy at delivery size.
