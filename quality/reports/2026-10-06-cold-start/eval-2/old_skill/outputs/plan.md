# Ceramic grinder launch — concrete production plan

A faithful final needs the real product and verified model name and price. With only After Effects open and no supplied product media or generation tool, I can define the shot and build an explicitly labeled timing layout. I cannot produce a truthful slow orbit of the actual grinder from nothing.

## Eight-second treatment

Assume a 1920×1080, 24 fps horizontal launch master until the delivery channel is specified. Black fills most of the frame. A narrow burnt-orange grazing light reveals the ceramic edge while a restrained neutral key preserves the real ceramic color and texture. The product is the largest and most detailed element; the final type is small relative to the object, but fully readable. Avoid making black ceramic look like chrome through sharp artificial highlights.

| Time | Shot and motion | Production requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1.25 s | Near-black silhouette. A soft orange edge reveals one side, then a neutral grazing light reveals ceramic texture. | Real material evidence; preserve the logo, surface imperfections and actual silhouette. |
| 1.25–4.75 s | Slow 15–20 degree move around the grinder, constant height, long-lens feel, subtle eased start and finish. One deliberate focal transition from ceramic texture toward the main body. | Real captured orbit/turntable footage or a dimensionally faithful textured 3D asset and rendering workflow. |
| 4.75–5.6 s | Camera settles on a three-quarter hero view. A faint grounding shadow holds the product in space; orange edge stays motivated from one side. | Matched lighting and real contact/shadow evidence. |
| 5.6–6.2 s | Verified model name reveals through a restrained mask, then verified price appears below. No bounce. | Exact copy, brand typeface or approved fallback, currency and any required qualifier. |
| 6.2–8 s | Still hero composition and fully legible name/price. Only extremely slow residual light movement if it improves material readability. | Review final crop and reading time at intended output size. |

## Minimum inputs needed

1. **Real product media.** Preferred: a high-quality 8–12-second slow orbit or turntable take of the exact product, with a clean hero hold, black or separable background and the planned lighting. Alternative: an accurate textured 3D model plus real reference photographs for shape, dimensions, ceramic finish, logo and controls. Also confirm rights to use the files.
2. **Verified product copy.** Exact model name, price, currency, target market, and whether tax, offer dates or “from” wording must accompany the price. No invented model or number will be presented as real.
3. **Delivery and brand essentials.** Landscape, portrait or square; intended placement; logo/typeface if required; color or finish variant; silent or sound-designed output.

A single supplied still can support a careful planar push and light treatment, but cannot reveal unseen sides in a faithful orbit. If a still is the only asset, revise the shot to a slow push across that real image and explicitly accept the changed camera move. Do not fabricate hidden geometry by rotating a flat cutout in 3D.

## Asset-to-AE workflow

For filmed material, capture enough resolution for reframing; maintain exposure and white balance during the orbit. Light the ceramic with a large soft key and a controlled orange strip/rim, plus negative fill for the black environment. Request a short gray/color reference capture and clean background plate when practical. Stable lighting is easier to grade than recover later.

For a 3D route, inspect model fidelity, UVs, logo placement, ceramic roughness and scale first. Confirm the installed AE version and its supported renderer/import path before committing to native rendering. If the model's required materials cannot be reproduced faithfully in that path, render beauty, alpha/shadow and useful lighting passes in a suitable 3D workflow, then composite in AE. AE alone does not guarantee arbitrary 3D assets render correctly. A camera move must follow actual geometry.

In AE, preserve the original linked media. Separate product treatment, background, grounding shadow, typography and final grade. Use editable native type/masks and semantic layer names. Set the copy on a quiet area of the frame, with safe margins and strong contrast. Use a measured soft highlight rolloff and preserve ceramic texture instead of crushing all dark tones. Never paint over real branding or use a fake product silhouette as the finished object.

A timing board can be made immediately with a neutral labeled stand-in and `[MODEL NAME]` / `[PRICE + CURRENCY]` copy. Label the entire board “PREVIS — PRODUCT AND COPY PENDING”. This proves pacing and layout only, not product fidelity or final material quality. No final export is represented as ready until the required media and factual copy arrive.

## Verification and delivery

Inspect silhouette, finish, edge treatment and label placement against real reference at the start, hero move midpoint and final hold. Check the orbit has no invented side details, matte chatter, clipped highlights, implausible reflections or sliding contact shadow. Review actual frames at 0.75, 2.5, 4.75, 6.2 and 7.8 seconds plus playback at speed. Check exact supplied text and price and allow at least 1.8 seconds of completely settled final copy.

Deliver an editable `.aep`, all referenced assets, review encode and agreed master after visual review. Verify eight-second duration, output dimensions, frame rate, codec and complete decode.
