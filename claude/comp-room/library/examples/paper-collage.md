# Native collage example: an invitation assembled from seed and paper

This example is a self-contained construction recipe, not a bundled AEP or a claim of finished-film approval. It uses only native AE shapes and editable text. No generated image, Content path, private rig or paid asset is needed.

**Input.** A fictional community garden invitation in a 1080×1920 portrait comp: “Grow something together.”, “The community garden”, then “Bring a seed. Meet a neighbor.” The viewer should understand that one seed becomes an invitation to join other people. Cream ground, deep green ink and mustard accent are choices for this example, not required style colors.

**Design.** A mustard seed is the persistent object. It lands on a paper strip, then a cut green stem and leaves unfold from it. One neighboring plant arrives; a second follows. Their overlap conveys a growing group, while a torn cream label carries the garden name. The final invitation appears on a broad paper card with enough settled time to read. The camera stays still and every piece has a clear order of assembly. Keep the original seed visible through the transition so the story has continuity.

**Native mechanism.** Use `AESCollage.paper(comp, {owner, name, width, height, color, texture, border, shadow, intensity, stepHz, seed})` for cards and strips. Use AE vector paths for the seed, stems and leaves; use `treatCutout` only on explicit owned non-text shapes that need edge/shadow and sampled motion. A 12 Hz, low-intensity boil can make a single leaf feel handled; other pieces can use intensity zero. Animate labels and copy independently at the full comp frame rate. Keep all text native and verify the installed font and complete copy.

**Correction to look for.** A leaf that crosses the label can make the words hard to read. Fix the overlap or label placement and re-render the crossing frames. If a shadow makes two cardstock pieces fuse, reduce softness/distance or stagger the pieces; don't merely increase texture. When a strip is built from repeated pieces, inspect every join for a visible seam. Preserve source art placement across a cut or reveal.

**Review.** Inspect the first readable headline, seed landing, label overlap, hardest handoff and final hold at full resolution and normal speed. Verify native shape/text editability, full movie decode, source/rights record and any correction. This written example is usable without opening a private quality report; a dated acceptance report should separately state what an actual run proved.

**Optional photographed-paper upgrade.** If the native surface does not meet the material brief, replace the cardstock visuals with rights-cleared transparent paper stocks and cutouts. First leave copy as native AE text and use the `paper-collage-recipes` blend/edge/grain method. If a physical handwritten look is still essential, its optional handwritten-paper plate route can generate exact-copy lettering in separate paper assets. Keep the native Source Text as an explicit copy record, reveal each printed card over blank stock, and check exact spelling plus every reveal. Visible lettering then requires regeneration/relinking after copy edits. This asset-dependent example does not change the generator-free native example above or its editability claims.

## A type-led native-paper announcement

This is a second adaptable pattern for an announcement with expressive type and no external imagery. It is a material-role guide, not a required layout or palette. In the MS-07 comparative revision, the strongest native result used a visible ground, two imperfect sheet edges that met at a seam, a contrasting paper backer, and a small joining/tape piece. The same repaired sheet remained under the event name and invitation. The weaker runs used smooth polygons or let the final sheet disappear, leaving type on a blank ground.

Choose one physical action that relates the subject to the invitation: join, unfold, reveal, peel or place. Give the main sheet a visible boundary against the ground at delivery size. Use `AESCollage.paper` for a primary sheet or an equivalent native stack with an irregular contour, subtle surface variation, coherent contact/offset shadow and a separate backer or join. Native paper need not imitate a photograph, but a perfectly smooth solid with a generic shadow is a blockout for a tactile brief. Move the sheet and its native text in a common coordinate space; keep a surviving piece through the final hold.

Start by proving one treated sheet over the final ground in a rendered still; do this before building the full motion sequence. View its center, cut edge and shadow at 100% and at ordinary playback size. If it looks like a clean polygon, correct the surface and depth first. After animation and encode, inspect the final invitation frame again: both readable copy and visible stock texture/shadow separation are required. In the MS-07 focused rerun, a readable CTA on flat polygons still failed the material criterion.

Measure installed-font text bounds before placing the tape, scraps and accent marks. Let a transition temporarily cover text only if the words become fully clear by the settled reading pose. In the final invitation, the named event remains readable and the CTA sits on an identifiable paper surface with a clear foreground/backing relationship. Inspect first-readable, seam midpoint, title hold and final hold both full-size and at viewing size. If the final paper edge vanishes against the ground, increase shape/tonal separation or correct the shadow and backer; adding more tiny scraps will not solve it. This pattern is generator-free and keeps the type editable.

**Final-pose clearance test.** Treat the event name and invitation as two measured text rectangles at the actual final keyframe. Reserve a separate visible paper region for each rectangle, with a gutter between them. Keep every foreground sheet, fold, tape, tab and accent outside both rectangles throughout the entire settled hold; an overlay on even the last word of the event name is a failed layout. Put the join behind the text or inside the empty gutter. For a 1920×1080 side-by-side composition, one workable starting layout is event-name bounds wholly within x=250–800, invitation bounds wholly within x=1000–1700, and the joining detail within x=850–950. Adapt the numbers to actual font metrics and leave clear margins; never force longer copy into those sample bounds. Render the delivered movie at the start and end of the final hold, view both at full size, and revise the layer order or geometry until both complete strings remain unobstructed. A nice paper texture does not compensate for a clipped or covered word.
