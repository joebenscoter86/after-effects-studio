# Motion language: the reusable foundation

Use this foundation for every style. The user's subject, brand and desired tone choose the appearance; the plugin supplies design reasoning and native execution. No personal rig, source repository, commercial font or generation service is required. The supporting documents preserve production detail without making one creator's look universal.

## 1. One visual argument per beat

State the primary message and what the viewer should notice first, second and last. Describe the picture with an active verb and an observable change: collected water feeds a plant; scattered entries become a sorted set; a label reveals the part it names. “Premium motion” or “three animated cards” is not yet an argument. Start from the actual subject or evidence before inventing a metaphor.

Give essential type, proof and artwork deliberate space. A guide, badge or accent earns its place by showing a relationship or emphasizing meaning. Remove decoration that crosses words or competes with the subject. A small amount of well-staged motion can carry a quiet editorial piece; a dense construction can suit an energetic explainer.

## 2. Design the connection before separate scenes

Write one concept sentence about the evolving picture, then decide what survives each boundary and what it becomes. A collected object can travel to its use; a detail can reveal its larger setting; a shape can become the receiving surface. Choose the next composition to make that relationship visible, rather than adding a wipe to unrelated finished layouts.

Continuity does not require a moving camera. A shared object, matching shape, stable framing or deliberate cut can connect the beats. Use a cut for a meaningful change of time, viewpoint, argument or mode. Explain that choice in the brief. `camera-continuity` describes when each mechanism fits and how to register its hand-off.

For a causal explainer, plan the source, carrier and recipient as one visible action before designing separate attractive scenes. The collected thing must remain identifiable as it leaves its source, travels or crosses a matched cut, and changes the recipient. A line pointing toward the recipient or a new droplet appearing later is not proof of transfer. If the chosen transition cannot show that relationship clearly in intermediate frames, simplify the geography or use a deliberate cut that preserves the same object and its destination.

Make the transfer legible as a change of state at both ends. In a rain-to-garden example, the visible water level can fall in the collector as the same colored volume travels through a connected channel, then the soil or plant responds on contact. A full collector beside a suddenly growing plant leaves the cause missing. This is an example of source change → carried action → recipient change, not a requirement to use channels or fluids in other briefs.

Treat a visible route as a physical claim. Its endpoints must touch the source outlet and recipient, and the carried object must remain on it through the middle frames. If registration is uncertain, omit the line and make the object travel directly or use a matched cut that keeps its identity. Never leave a disconnected guide in the delivered movie merely because it makes the composition look more designed.

## 3. Construct, act, resolve

Compose start, action and resolution poses, including the hardest overlap. Establish the space, introduce the acting subject, show a consequential change, then hold the result long enough to understand. Construction order should reveal how the picture works. Do not add lines merely because a reference used lines, or animate a guide that misses the object it introduces.

Keep a recognizable anchor where useful: the same object, a data value, a label or the source selection. Its identity matters more than a mandatory mascot. Supporting actions can overlap while one thing leads attention. Reading holds may be still; drift is a choice, not a requirement.

## 4. Give motion a purpose and intensity

Translation carries; rotation exposes; scale changes emphasis or scope; occlusion explains depth; contact completes an action. Name the purpose and receiving object. Separate the scene's movement from text that must remain readable. Do not add a zoom to every word or apply position, scale, rotation and opacity to every layer by habit.

Choose restrained, moderate or energetic intensity from the subject and audience. `construction` retains measured timing/easing examples, labeled as examples. Convert their seconds to the actual composition frame rate. Event counts, spring constants and house colors are not universal quality rules; identity, exact copy, safe ownership and honest verification are requirements.

## 5. Plan type and material together

Assign each line a role: main statement, emphasis, explanation, caption or evidence. Preserve exact approved copy. Choose line breaks and useful contrast before reducing font size. Check installed resolved faces, glyphs, actual bounds and delivery-scale readability; long copy may require more space or time. See `type-evidence`.

Native AE owns exact text, data, paths and editable motion. Native geometry is appropriate for an intentional diagram or paper construction. Supplied or cleared art can provide complex physical material. Inspect assets before promising a look; missing premium product art calls for a suitable fallback or an explicit blockout, not a crude placeholder presented as finished. See `assets-depth`. Brand styling is optional and separate from the motion mechanism.

## 6. Build the middle, then inspect the actual result

For a new visual system, test a hard hand-off and an ordinary explanatory beat. Define source/destination bounds, pivot, coordinate space, carried layers and visibility frames. Read the current native schema, preserve unrelated work and inspect uncertain completion before retrying. `construction` covers rest transforms and parenting; `failure-atlas` maps observed failures to checks.

Render first-readable, action, overlap, hold and seam frames. Inspect full-size type and decoded frames from the delivered movie as well as native captures. Fix the cause of collisions, detached shadows and broken registration. If the visual idea is wrong, revise the brief rather than stacking local effects. Full decode proves file integrity; full-speed playback checks rhythm and audio. State any unavailable check.

## Reading path and delivery

Read `production-workflow` and `brief-template`, then only relevant documents: `construction`, `camera-continuity`, `assets-depth`, `type-evidence`, `reference-study`, `worked-scenes` or `failure-atlas`. Apply `quality` before delivery. The installed library is self-contained; its provenance paths are historical references.

Deliver the editable project and verified requested export. Keep technical validation, visual judgment and user acceptance separate. Preserve reusable decisions with timings and evidence in the user's project. A successful build, a favorable still or one accepted scene does not approve a whole film or publication.
