# Motion language: the reusable foundation

This is the default craft system for After Effects Studio. It carries the production decisions developed in Joe Benscoter's Content pipeline into projects with different subjects, brands and aspect ratios. Read it at the beginning of every motion-design task. The user's brief chooses the look; this language determines how to reason about the animation.

## 1. Start with an operation the viewer can understand

Describe a scene using an active verb and an observable before/after: a message is carried into a draft; a pile is scanned into an organized set; a container opens to reveal its structure; a value changes as material leaves. “Make screenshots fluid,” “add premium motion,” or “animate three cards” does not yet describe the idea.

Choose a specific subject, a primary action and a payoff. The strongest candidate is often already in the narration or product workflow. Start literal before inventing an unrelated metaphor. If source material already contains the action, compose and emphasize that evidence; do not bury it under decorative graphics.

## 2. Give attention a stable object

Choose the scene's star: an identifiable object, label, piece of evidence or character. It can leave and return; its return should pay off an earlier setup. Keep its identity legible while the environment changes. A recognizable object crossing a boundary communicates continuity more clearly than a sequence of unrelated cards.

A recurring star is a storytelling technique, not a requirement to add a mascot to every project. A chart's value, a real reply, or a product silhouette can be the star. Keep tiny bright characters away from evidence and reading areas.

## 3. Construct, act, resolve

Build a picture from related parts over time. Establish the surface or guide, reveal structure, introduce the hero, add its meaningful detail, change its state, then hold the result. Supporting movements overlap without competing for attention. Repeating the same entrance on a collection of finished pictures does not implement this language.

For explanatory construction sequences, retain the detailed measured grammar in `construction`: line first, material resolution, delayed accent, a distinct snap, a shared hand-off. For a slower title or cinematic product shot, retain causal staging and hierarchy while changing density and timing. Universal means the mechanism transfers; it does not mean reducing it to “use easing.”

## 4. Make movement earn its place

Translation carries; rotation reveals; scale changes emphasis or space; occlusion establishes depth; exact contact completes an action; a camera move connects two ideas. Name that purpose in the brief. If the movement has no subject or outcome, revise it before adding keys.

Separate object motion from camera motion. Fast detail can happen inside a readable, stable composition. The camera has its own slower rhythm. Do not attach a new zoom to every spoken word. Selective stillness and restrained drift are both valid holds; state which you intend.

## 5. Design the middle and the landing

Compose start, action and payoff poses for the hardest passage. Include the actual intermediate overlap and destination registration. The middle is where duplicate text, clipping, ambiguous depth and unexplained arcs hide. Prose alone rarely reveals these problems.

For a source-to-destination transfer, specify source rectangle, pivot, trajectory, scale, rotation, destination bounds and exactly when the source copy disappears. If the source is evidence, preserve its meaning and identity. Do not animate a user instruction into a supposed product result or a draft into proof of a sent message.

## 6. Material quality and precision work together

Native AE owns exact type, data, paths, masks, keyframes, UI and compositing. Supplied, licensed or generated art can supply sophisticated surfaces and recognizable physical objects. These assets should be prepared for motion: clean alpha, consistent viewpoint, no baked shadow when AE owns the lighting, and separate moving pieces.

Do not substitute a rectangle for a requested premium physical object and call it finished. A native document, chart, circuit or intentionally geometric diagram is a legitimate subject. If the brief requires a polished product asset and none is available, resolve that dependency or show a clearly labeled blocking study. See `assets-depth`.

## 7. Typography carries structure

Use a held section title to establish the idea when that fits the format. Essential words survive long enough to read. Subtitles support speech; they should not compete with title, proof and hero for the same area. Long copy calls for a layout decision, not indiscriminate shrinking. Measure rendered bounds at delivery size and during camera pushes.

## 8. Critique the artifact and revise the brief when needed

Build a representative difficult section before scaling a new visual system across the whole piece. Include an ordinary explanatory beat as well as a hero or transition: one excellent moment does not prove the rest. Inspect rendered frames, dense transition samples and a playable clip. Technical success, visual quality and the user's approval are separate claims.

When feedback reveals a wrong visual idea, rewrite the scene brief from the reference and intent. Repeatedly patching the named symptom can preserve the wrong design for several rounds. Capture accepted techniques with exact parameters and evidence in the user's project so future work benefits.
