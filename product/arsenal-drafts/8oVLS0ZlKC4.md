## Soft one-way blurred dark shapes with a bright stroke fringe on a pale ground
id: 01-blurred-stroked-shapes-on-light-ground
status: unproven
source: 8oVLS0ZlKC4 13.76–61.52 s
jobs: depth and finish; glow and colour; lines and shapes
what it does for a beat: Turns two flat, crude shapes into a soft, lens-like backdrop in under a minute. Reach for it when a title or scene needs an organic, out-of-focus base layer rather than hard vector art.
what it looks like: Two huge near-black navy circles, one pushing in from above the frame and one from below, leave an hourglass of pale ice-blue light between them. Their edges are smeared vertically into wide soft gradients, and a saturated blue stroke on the circles shows as a soft blue band, about 70 comp px tall, where dark meets light.
ingredients:
- Shape layers drawn with the Ellipse tool — built-in; toolbar Ellipse tool (shown f001259)
- Solid layer for the background — built-in; Layer > New > Solid (inferred; the Solid Settings dialog is shown f002050)
- Gaussian Blur — stock; found by typing in the Effects & Presets panel, where it sits under Blur & Sharpen (shown f002389)
- Shape stroke — built-in; set from the toolbar Stroke swatch and width with both shape layers selected (shown f003664)
steps:
1. With the Ellipse tool, draw a very large circle whose lower edge dips into the top of the frame; zoom the viewer well out (about 12–15 %) so it can extend far past the edges (drawing said; the zoom is shown only, f001626)
   - Fill: a very dark blue, close to black (said "darkest blue"; the sampled fill reads about RGB 2, 11, 23 — inferred from pixels)
   - Stroke: none at this stage (shown f002948, toolbar Stroke swatch crossed out)
2. Draw a second, equally large circle whose upper edge rises into the bottom of the frame, so the two leave a curved hourglass gap in the middle (said; shown f002152, where both circles have landed; at f001626 the second one is still being dragged out)
   - Size and place of both circles: no numbers are given; measured on f002152 each is about 3520 px across on the 1920 x 1080 comp, the upper one centred near (943, -1298) and the lower one near (944, 2351) (see `layout`)
3. Add a solid that fills the comp, set its colour to a very pale blue and put it at the bottom of the stack (said; shown f002152)
   - Color: R 228, G 244, B 255 (#E4F4FF) (shown f001970)
   - Size: 1920 x 1080, same as comp (shown f002050)
4. Apply Gaussian Blur to the lower circle (said; shown f002948)
   - Blurriness: 103.6 (said "around 100"; shown f002948)
   - Blur Dimensions: Vertical (shown f002948; the menu is opened from the default Horizontal and Vertical at f002700 and Vertical is picked by f002800). The narrator suggests trying both one-way options; only Vertical is shown being chosen (said / shown)
   - Repeat Edge Pixels: off — the author calls this the most important part (said; shown f002948)
5. Give the upper circle the same Gaussian Blur with the same three settings (shown f003441; how it was copied is not shown — copy and paste of the effect is inferred)
6. Select both circle layers and give them a bright blue stroke from the toolbar (said "light colour"; shown f003664)
   - Stroke Width: 9 px (shown f003664; it passes through 10 px while being dragged in f003441)
   - Stroke Color: R 0, G 130, B 255 (#0082FF) (shown in the Shape Stroke Color dialog at source 60.5 s, frame 3630, as OK is pressed; the landed blue swatch is shown f003664). The narrator says a light colour; the screen shows a saturated mid blue, and the screen wins. A paler blue (about #5598D8) is tried just before (shown at source 59.5 s).
layout: each circle is about 1.83 times the picture width across (about 3520 comp px, 3.26 times the height), so both overhang the left and right edges by a long way (measured: circles fitted to the hard edges on f002152, after both have landed and before any blur, fit error under 1 px; circles fitted to the half-brightness contour of the result frame give the same, 3470–3530 px). Upper circle centred at about (943, -1298) comp px: 49 % across, 1.20 picture heights above the top edge, its lowest point at 463 px (42.9 % of the height). Lower circle centred at about (944, 2351): 49 % across, 1.18 heights below the bottom edge, its highest point at 590 px (54.6 %) (measured on f002152). An earlier figure of 1.5 times the width was read from f001626, where the second circle is still being dragged out; it is wrong. The pale gap at half brightness, the same before and after the blur: 43–55 % of the height at the centre, 42–56 % at 40 % across, 37–61 % at 25 %, 27–71 % at 10 %, 18–80 % at the left edge (measured on f002152 and on the result cut at 61.333 s; the gap is mirrored left to right). On the result: the dark masses read about RGB 2–4, 10–11, 23 and the gap about 226–228, 242, 255 (the E4F4FF solid after compression); the dark-to-pale edge rises from 10 % to 90 % over about 86–89 comp px (8 % of the height) at each point measured from 1 % to 40 % across, which is what Blurriness 103.6 gives; the stroke's blue band (rows where blue exceeds red by more than 60 levels) is about 67–80 comp px tall on each edge, blue minus red up to about 90, against about 50 without the stroke on f002948 — so the blue fringe comes from the blurred stroke, not from the blur alone. The top and bottom frame edges stay fully dark (no fade), as a vertical blur on oversized shapes with Repeat Edge Pixels off gives (measured)
timing and easing: none given (nothing is animated in this part; the motion comes in technique 02)
variations: any shapes can stand in for the circles (said); either one-way blur direction can be used, swapping between Horizontal and Vertical for a different distortion (said)
pitfalls: leaving Repeat Edge Pixels on is the thing to avoid — it is stressed as the key setting (said); no other warnings given
longest on: the Gaussian Blur step, about 15 s (36.8–52.2 s), against about 8 s drawing the circles, 9 s on the background and 9 s on the stroke; within it the author stresses switching Repeat Edge Pixels off
build notes: Shape layers: comp.layers.addShape(), add "ADBE Vector Group" > "ADBE Vector Shape - Ellipse" (Size about [3520, 3520] on a 1920 x 1080 comp, measured — no value is given in the video; place the upper circle's centre at about [943, -1298] and the lower's at about [944, 2351] in comp space, by the ellipse Position or the layer Position), "ADBE Vector Graphic - Fill" (Color) and "ADBE Vector Graphic - Stroke" (Color, Stroke Width). Background: comp.layers.addSolid([228/255,244/255,1], name, 1920, 1080, 1) moved to the bottom. Effect: layer.Effects.addProperty("ADBE Gaussian Blur 2"); properties "Blurriness" = 103.6, "Blur Dimensions" = 3 (1 Horizontal and Vertical, 2 Horizontal, 3 Vertical), "Repeat Edge Pixels" = 0. All of this can be done by script; nothing here needs the interface. Comp: The composition reads 1920 x 1080 at 60 fps (solid "100% of comp" at 1920 x 1080, shown f002050; 60.00 fps under the timecode, shown f002152). Comparison crop: the frame is cut from source.mp4 at 61.333 s (saved as scratch/fix-01/r61.333.png). Both circle layers stay selected to the end of this part, so their anchor and path handles sit at the centre of the picture; the crop is the left 47 % of the picture, which holds the blurred edges, stroke fringe and pale gap without handles or the mouse. The whole viewer picture at that moment spans about x 800–2572, y 381–1374.
result: start=61.2 end=61.5 still crop=806,384,834,990 frame=f003680
before: f002152 (the two hard-edged circles on the pale solid, no blur, no stroke)
unknowns: the exact fill colour of the dark circles (only sampled from pixels); the circles' size and position as typed values (only measured); how the blur was copied to the second circle; the stroke's line join and other stroke options. (The blur is a layer effect, so it always acts on the stroke too; the order of stroke and fill inside the shape does not change that.)

## Wiggled glow, darkest radial blur and fine grain finish for bright scenes
id: 02-glow-radial-blur-noise-finish-stack
status: unproven
source: 8oVLS0ZlKC4 61.52–127.12 s
jobs: glow and colour; depth and finish; texture
what it does for a beat: A three-adjustment-layer finish that makes any soft, light-backed composition bloom and gain film grit, with wiggle expressions on the glow radius and blur amount as its only source of movement. Use it over a blurred or abstract backdrop (in the tutorial, the blurred shapes of technique 01, whose figures are repeated in step 1) when the frame feels static and digital.
what it looks like: The pale gap between two dark soft forms blooms into a near-white, faintly cyan glow whose edges turn a wider, more electric blue; near the left and right frame edges the dark masses are smeared a little further into the gap by the radial blur; a very fine monochrome grain sits over everything. The glow radius and blur amount are wiggled by expression, but the comp is never played in this part, so the motion is not seen (see `timing and easing`).
ingredients:
- Adjustment layers (three) — built-in; Layer > New > Adjustment Layer, Ctrl+Alt+Y on Windows (menu shown f004024)
- Glow — stock (Stylize category; applied from the Effects & Presets search, inferred)
- CC Radial Fast Blur — stock (Blur & Sharpen category, inferred)
- Noise — stock; Noise & Grain > Noise in the Effects & Presets panel (shown f007370)
- wiggle() expressions — built-in; Alt-click the property's stopwatch (said)
steps:
1. Start from a composition with a bright, pale background and soft dark shapes over it: the result of 01 (shown f003664). To stand alone, 01's base in figures (all from recipe 01, shown or measured there):
   - Comp 1920 x 1080, 60 fps; a comp-sized solid at bottom, #E4F4FF
   - Two circles about 3520 px across (1.83 times the comp width), fill near-black navy (about RGB 2, 11, 23), centred at about (943, -1298) and (944, 2351), so the pale gap between them runs 43–55 % of the height at the centre, 37–61 % at 25 % across, 27–71 % at 10 % and 18–80 % at the left edge
   - On each circle: Gaussian Blur, Blurriness 103.6, Blur Dimensions Vertical, Repeat Edge Pixels off; stroke 9 px, #0082FF
   - 01's result, to compare against: dark-to-pale edge 10–90 % over about 86–89 comp px; blue band (blue exceeding red by 60+ levels) about 67–80 comp px on each edge
2. Add an adjustment layer on top of the stack (said; shown f004024 for the menu, f005197 for the layer)
3. Apply Glow to it and push the threshold to the top and the radius very high, because the background is bright (said; shown f005197)
   - Glow Based On: Color Channels (shown f005197)
   - Glow Threshold: 100.0 % (said "max out"; shown f005197). By script this control may read 0 to 255 rather than percent (LESSONS 17): set it, read it back, and use 255 if 100 reads back as about 39 %
   - Glow Radius: 331 (said "highly increase"; shown f005197)
   - Glow Intensity: 0.3 (said "lower the intensity"; shown f005704)
   - Composite Original: Behind; Glow Operation: Add; Glow Colors: Original Colors; Color Looping: Triangle A>B>A; Color Loops: 1.0 (left at defaults; shown f005197, f005704)
   - Glow Dimensions: reads "Horizontal..." truncated; taken to be the default Horizontal and Vertical (inferred from f005704)
4. Alt-click the Glow Radius stopwatch and enter a wiggle expression (said; shown f005704)
   - Glow Radius expression: wiggle(2,100) (shown f005704)
5. Duplicate the Glow effect on the same adjustment layer, so a second Glow with the same settings and the same radius expression sits under the first (said "duplicate the effect"; shown f005926, where the copy reads Threshold 100.0 %, Intensity 0.3 and an expression-driven radius)
   - At f005926 (comp time 0:00:04:28) the first Glow's radius reads 289.0 and the second's 319.5. This is the same wiggle drawing different random values in each copy, not a changed setting: leave both expressions as they are (shown f005926)
6. Add a second, separate adjustment layer above the first (said; shown f006133)
7. Apply CC Radial Fast Blur to it (said; settings shown f006937, where the pointer is on the effect's on/off switch; the author switches it off and on around 115.5–115.75 s, and it is on again at 118.6 s and in the result)
   - Center: 960.0, 540.0, the comp centre (default; shown f006937)
   - Amount: 95.0 (said "around 95"; shown f006937)
   - Zoom: Darkest, chosen because the background is bright (said; shown f006937; the menu reads Standard, Brightest, Darkest in that order, shown open at source 114.25 s; Standard leaves a grey radial haze over the dark masses there)
8. Alt-click the Amount stopwatch and add a wiggle (said; shown f007138, read from a crop of the timeline panel)
   - Amount expression: wiggle(2,10) (shown f007138)
9. Optional: add a third adjustment layer on top and apply Noise for grit (said "if you want"; shown f007610)
   - Amount of Noise: 9.0 % (shown f007610)
   - Use Color Noise: off, unticked from its default of on (shown f007610; still on at source 124.8 s, off from 125.0 s)
   - Clip Result Values: on (shown f007610)
layout: base picture as in step 1 (from 01). The adjustment layers are full-frame, comp-sized — the noise layer's handles sit on the picture's corners and edge midpoints at 124.6 s and the glow layer's at f005197 (measured); the radial-blur layer's size is never shown and is taken to be the same (inferred); the CC Radial Fast Blur centre is at the comp centre, 50 % / 50 % of the picture (measured from Center 960, 540 on a 1920 x 1080 comp, f006937). Measured on the result (cut at 125.2 s, picture x 759–2418, y 368–1301 full size), against 01's result at the same places:
   - Gap at half brightness: 19–78 % of the height at the left edge, 26–71 % at 10 % across, 36–62 % at 25 %, 41–57 % at 40 % (01: 18–80, 27–71, 37–61, 42–56): the gap widens slightly in the middle and narrows slightly at the frame edges (measured)
   - Dark masses stay about RGB 2–3, 11–12, 23–24; in the gap's core red is unchanged at about 223–226 (95 % of core pixels read 226 or less), green rises from about 242 to 251–252 and blue stays at about 252–254, so it reads near-white with a faint cyan cast (measured)
   - Edge softness, 10–90 % rise: about 126 comp px at the left edge, 116 at 10 % across, 104 at 25 %, 89 at 40 % (01: 86–89 everywhere): the softening grows towards the frame edges (measured)
   - Blue fringe: blue exceeds red by up to about 130–158 levels (01: about 90), and the band where it exceeds 60 levels is about 105–135 comp px tall on each edge (01: 67–80) (measured)
   - The radial blur alone, measured with it switched on (115.5 s) and off (115.75 s) at the same view, Noise not yet added: at the left edge it narrows the gap from 18–80 % to 19.5–78 % and softens the edge from 95 to 123 comp px; at 25 % and 40 % across it changes neither by more than half a percent or 5 px. So its pull is only visible towards the frame edges, growing with distance from the centre (measured)
   - The rest of the change from 01 (core lift, wider blue band, softer edges in the middle) is the two Glows (inferred from the on/off pair above)
   - Grain from Noise 9 % is barely measurable in the recording (a fraction of a level of fine deviation); it is a texture, not a visible tone change (measured)
timing and easing: no keyframes; all motion would come from wiggle expressions — wiggle(2,100) on both Glow Radius values and wiggle(2,10) on the radial blur Amount (shown f005704, f005926, f007138). The comp is never played in this part: the playhead is parked at 0:00:00:00 for f005704, f007610 and the result, and around 91–93 s, 98.5–100.5 s (f005926) and 120–120.5 s the author only scrubs it back and forth (comp frames 232, 477, 149, 589, 165 within two seconds of video, then 232, 350, 151, 250, then 317, 474), never at playing speed, so the readouts there (radius 289.0 and 319.5 at 0:00:04:28) are values passed through, not keys. What the settings imply: a new random target twice a second, so each swell or fall takes about half a second, not a slow breathing; the Glow Radius swings up to about 100 either side of 331 (roughly 230–430, about plus or minus 30 %), independently in each Glow copy; the radial blur Amount swings up to about 10 either side of 95 (about plus or minus 11 %).
variations: on a bright background the glow wants maximum threshold and the radial blur wants Darkest (said); by implication a dark background would use other settings, but none are given here; the Noise layer is optional (said)
pitfalls: too much glow intensity overpowers the image, so keep it low (said); none other given
longest on: the Glow layer, about 41 s (61.5–102.9 s), including about 16 s on the wiggle and the duplicate; CC Radial Fast Blur gets about 16 s (102.9–119.0 s) and Noise about 8 s (119.0–127.1 s)
build notes: Each adjustment layer: comp.layers.addSolid([1,1,1], name, comp.width, comp.height, 1) then layer.adjustmentLayer = true, added so it sits above the scene. Glow: Effects.addProperty("ADBE Glo2"); set "Glow Threshold" (100 %), "Glow Radius" (331), "Glow Intensity" (0.3); for the threshold's scale by script see step 3. Then glowRadius.expression = "wiggle(2,100)"; the duplicate is easiest as effect.duplicate() or a second addProperty with the same values and expression. CC Radial Fast Blur: matchName "CC Radial Fast Blur"; "Center" [960,540], "Amount" 95 with expression "wiggle(2,10)", "Zoom" menu = Darkest (index 3: the open menu shows Standard, Brightest, Darkest in that order at source 114.25 s). Noise: "ADBE Noise"; "Amount of Noise" 9, "Use Color Noise" 0, "Clip Result Values" 1. Layer order from bottom: scene, glow layer, radial-blur layer, noise layer (shown f008910). Comparison crop: frame cut from source.mp4 at 125.2 s (scratch/fix-02/r125.2.png); the noise adjustment layer is selected, so its handles sit on the picture's edges and centre — the crop is a left-side inset (about 43 % of the width) that avoids them; the whole viewer picture spans about x 759–2418, y 368–1301 at that time. The settings wiggle, so a build at another comp time will differ a little; compare the overall level, gap shares and edge colour, not exact pixels. The mouse arrow flickers into the crop at 125.1, 125.3 and 125.4 s, so the clean stretch is only about frames 7507–7514 (125.12–125.23 s), checked frame by frame.
result: start=125.12 end=125.23 still crop=790,400,730,850 frame=f007512
before: f003664 (the blurred, stroked shapes before any adjustment layer)
unknowns: whether the second Glow differs in any value (only threshold, intensity and an expression-driven radius are readable); how the Glow effect was found (likely the Effects & Presets search); whether anything was changed on the Glow Colors or Color A/B (they read Original Colors, white and black); blending modes of the adjustment layers (none changed on screen); noise defaults beyond those three values

## Soft pockets of light from heavily blurred edge shapes
id: 03-blurred-light-shapes-at-frame-edges
status: unproven
source: 8oVLS0ZlKC4 127.12–147.12 s (the expression is finished and the result shown just after, up to 148.6 s)
jobs: shadows and light; depth and finish; glow and colour
what it does for a beat: Adds soft patches of lighter tone around the edges of an otherwise finished frame (with a wiggle on one blur for movement), so the dark areas are not flat and the eye is pulled toward the centre. A quick pass to make a background feel lit and alive.
what it looks like: Inside the dark navy masses at the top and bottom of the frame, faint blue shafts and glows appear: one hazy patch spread both sideways and downwards at the top centre, and two alike vertical light columns with fairly crisp sides rising from the bottom edge and fading upwards. The base is the finished hourglass of 01 and 02 (two blurred dark circles with a glowing pale gap). One blur carries a wiggle, but the comp is never played in this part, so no motion is seen.
ingredients:
- Shape layers drawn with the Ellipse tool — built-in (shown f008030)
- Gaussian Blur — stock; Blur & Sharpen (shown f008840)
- wiggle() expression — built-in (said; shown f008887)
steps:
0. Start from the finished look of 01 and 02 (shown f007790). To stand alone, its figures (from recipes 01 and 02, shown or measured there):
   - Comp 1920 x 1080, 60 fps; comp-sized solid #E4F4FF at the bottom
   - Two circles about 3520 px across, fill about RGB 2, 11, 23, centred at about (943, -1298) and (944, 2351); each with Gaussian Blur 103.6, Vertical, Repeat Edge Pixels off, and a 9 px #0082FF stroke
   - Above them three comp-sized adjustment layers, bottom to top: Glow (Color Channels, Threshold 100 %, Radius 331 with wiggle(2,100), Intensity 0.3) plus a duplicate of it; CC Radial Fast Blur (Center 960, 540, Amount 95 with wiggle(2,10), Zoom Darkest); Noise (9 %, colour noise off, Clip Result Values on)
   - That base measures on this result frame (148.5 s): pale gap at half brightness 20–77 % of the height at the left edge, 27–70 % at 10 % across, 36–62 % at 25 %, 42–56 % at 40 %, 43–55 % at the centre; dark masses about RGB 3, 12, 24; gap core about 215–226, 238–252, 250–253; blue fringe band (blue over red by 60+ levels) about 100–145 comp px tall on each edge (measured)
1. Draw circles in a lighter blue than the dark shapes and place them so they are cut by the frame edges: one at the top centre and two along the bottom (said "around the sides"; shown f008030, f008270)
   - Fill: a mid blue, sampled at about RGB 53, 94, 160 (#355EA0) (inferred from pixels in f008030; the value is not shown)
   - Stroke: none (shown f008150, toolbar Stroke swatch crossed out; a Stroke Options dialog is used on the way, f007850)
2. Keep these new layers above the finishing adjustment layers from technique 02, so the glow, radial blur and noise do not act on them (shown f008150 and f008910 in the layer stack; not said). The result confirms it: the light columns keep fairly crisp sides with no radial smear or glow bloom (seen on the 148.5 s cut)
3. Give the circles Gaussian Blur with a very high blurriness, one-way on the bottom (said "vertical or horizontal"; shown f008840, f008750)
   - Bottom-left circle (its handles sit at about 18–37 % across on f008840): Blurriness 599.6, Blur Dimensions Vertical, Repeat Edge Pixels off (shown f008840). The value appears first at 103.6, the dark shapes' value, before being raised, so the blur was probably copied from them (inferred)
   - Top circle: Blurriness 555.6, a fixed value shown in blue, not expression-driven (shown f008750); Blur Dimensions: Horizontal and Vertical. The panel shows only a truncated "Horizontal..." (f008750), but the picture settles it: the top patch is about as soft downwards as sideways, fading smoothly over 20 % of the height with no step, where a Horizontal-only blur would leave a crisp lower edge about 12 % of the height below the top (the lower half of the scaled circle) (measured on the 148.5 s cut); Repeat Edge Pixels off (shown f008750)
   - Bottom-right circle: its settings are never shown (it has an effect, fx switch at f008750), but the picture answers them: its column is the same width as the left one (about 16 % across at half level), with sides as crisp (10–90 % over about 95 comp px, no sideways blur) and the same fall-off upwards, so give it the same Gaussian Blur, Blurriness about 600, Vertical, Repeat Edge Pixels off (measured on the 148.5 s cut). Whether it also carries the wiggle cannot be seen
4. Scale the top circle down (not said; shown f008840)
   - Scale: 70.0, 70.0 % (shown f008840); it was already at 75 % before (shown f008690, 75.0, 75.0 %), so it ends at 70 % of its drawn size, about 13 % of the picture width across before blurring
5. Alt-click the Blurriness stopwatch and add a wiggle so the light pockets move (said; shown f008887; the result is never played, see `timing and easing`)
   - Blurriness expression: wiggle(2,100) (shown f008887, on the bottom-left circle, being typed at f008840; the top circle's 555.6 shows in blue, so it has no expression)
6. In the finished state the three light circles are parented to the lower dark shape from technique 01 (shown f008910; done off camera and not explained)
layout: the base hourglass, its glow and grain fill about three quarters of the picture; its figures are in step 0. Each light circle is about 18 % of the picture width across before blurring (measured on f008150, about 132 px of a 714 px picture); the top circle is centred at about 48 % across with its centre on the top edge, then scaled to 70 % (about 13 % of the width; measured/shown f008840); the bottom circles are centred at about 28 % and 68 % across with their centres on the bottom edge, each cut in half (measured on f008150). Measured on the result, the 148.5 s cut (picture x 744–2386, y 156–1076 full size): the bottom columns are centred at about 27.4 % and 67.8 % across and run about 19–36 % and 60–76 % across at half level, both about 16 % wide near the bottom edge and keeping that width upwards; each side rises 10–90 % over about 92–95 comp px at the bottom edge; brightness above the dark mass (about 12–13 levels) falls from about 66 at the bottom edge to about 56, 45, 35 and 30 at 5, 10, 15 and 20 % of the height up, alike in both columns; at the bottom edge each column reads about RGB 40, 60, 98, roughly 60–75 % of the circle's fill per channel, which fits a circle centred on the edge with a vertical blur of about 600; the top patch peaks at about RGB 39, 55, 86 on the top edge, is centred at about 49 % across and runs 38–59 % across at half level (about 21 % of the width) and fades downwards from about 58 to 43 levels over the top 15 % of the height (measured)
timing and easing: no keyframes; the only motion would come from wiggle(2,100) on the bottom-left circle's Blurriness (shown f008887), plus the wiggles already on the 02 layers. The comp is never played in this part: the playhead stays parked at 0:00:04:48 from about 129.5 to 148.45 s and jumps to 0:00:05:00 for the result, where it stays. What the settings imply: a new random target twice a second (each change taking about half a second), with the blurriness swinging up to about 100 either side of its value of about 600, about plus or minus 17 %; so the left column's reach and brightness would change by roughly a sixth, quickly rather than slowly, while the top patch and (probably) the right column hold still
variations: Vertical or Horizontal can be chosen per shape (said); placement and number of shapes are free (inferred from "around the sides")
pitfalls: none given

## Turning a crude gradient disc into a grainy glowing orb
