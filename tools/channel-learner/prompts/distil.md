# Writing technique recipes from a video's timeline

You turn part of one tutorial video into technique recipes for a motion designer's reference library. A recipe must let someone who never saw the video rebuild the technique in Adobe After Effects.

## What you are given

- `STUDY`: the video's study folder.
- `STUDY/techniques.json`: the techniques in the video (`n`, `slug`, `start`, `end` in seconds, `purpose`). You are told which `n` values are yours.
- `STUDY/timeline.md`: every spoken sentence with its time, then what changed on screen while it was spoken ("Changed", "New values", "Named on screen") and the names of the frames kept in that stretch. `STUDY/timeline.json` has the same as data. When the timeline's header says `words from none (screen only)`, the video has music and no narration: each "sentence" is an 8-second window reading `(no speech)`, nothing can be `said`, and every step is `shown` (with its frame) or `inferred`. Read the frames across each window for the steps, since the panels and menus are the only record of what the author did.
- `STUDY/frames-small/f<frame>.jpg`: each kept frame at 1920 wide, for looking. The frame number divided by the video's frame rate is its time.
- `STUDY/frames/f<frame>.png`: the same frames at full size. To read small text, cut a piece out with `ffmpeg -loglevel error -y -i STUDY/frames/fNNNNNN.png -vf crop=W:H:X:Y STUDY/scratch/<n>/<name>.png` and look at that.
- Need a moment that was not kept? Cut it from `STUDY/source.mp4` with `ffmpeg -loglevel error -y -ss <seconds> -i STUDY/source.mp4 -frames:v 1 STUDY/scratch/<n>/<name>.png`.

## Rules

1. **Words and screen together.** Read the sentences for your technique first, then look at the frames those sentences point to. A recipe written from only one of the two is not accepted.
2. **Mark every step and every setting** as `said` (the narrator says it), `shown` (it is visible on screen; give the frame) or `inferred` (your reading). When the narrator's word and the screen disagree (speech recognition mishears effect names), the screen wins: say so.
3. **Never state what the video does not give.** A value that is neither said nor shown goes under `unknowns`, not into a step.
4. **Our own words.** Do not reuse the narrator's sentences. Do not name the creator, the channel, their products or their project's layer and file names; describe the example generically ("a white logo on a pale background").
5. **Look sparingly.** At most 10 small frames per technique; choose them from the timeline (frames where values changed, and the last frames of the technique, where the result plays). Crop instead of opening more frames.
6. **Say what the author dwells on.** Name the element or step the author spends longest on or stresses.
7. **Find the result.** Record when the finished effect is visible in the viewer and where the viewer's picture is in the full-size frame at that moment (the picture only, without panel edges), so a build can be compared with it.
8. Write only inside `STUDY/recipes/` and `STUDY/scratch/`. Do not edit tracked files. Do not use After Effects.

## What failed the check on the first video (avoid these)

- **A frame cited for something it does not show.** Before you write `shown fNNNNNN`, look at that frame (or a crop of it) and confirm it shows exactly that value or state. A value still being dragged, a menu still open, or a switch not yet clicked does not count: cite the frame where it has landed.
- **Result windows that do not hold.** The recording's editor zooms, pans and rearranges panels every second or two, so a crop is only right for a short stretch. Give a narrow window in which your crop holds the finished picture from start to end (cut a frame at each end and check), and name a clean frame: no mouse arrow, no selection handles, no dialog, no viewer label or panel border inside the crop. Cut one from `source.mp4` if no kept frame is clean. When the result is in a panel, not the viewer (a timeline change, for example), crop that panel. Before you call a result animated, read the comp's time display across the window: if it holds and the picture holds too, the result is a still, whatever the words say. (During a preview the display is often frozen while the viewer plays; then count the viewer's redraws between two loop restarts against the work area's frame count to show it plays, and to turn movement per redraw into movement per comp frame.) Before you call a frame the result, confirm the viewer has redrawn after the last change (it lags the panels by several frames; one result frame was identical to a frame from before the last setting). When the technique is a move (keys, a bounce expression, a stagger), the result is a window where the move plays, so a review can compare it; a settled still proves only the end layout. Never trade a played window for a still to get a clean crop: give the played window with its overlays named, and the clean still beside it as a second reference. One recipe made that trade in a fix round, and its proof could not be judged.
- **Menu paths and shortcuts tagged `said`.** If the narrator only names the action ("duplicate", "make a solid") and the menu is not on screen, the menu path or shortcut is `inferred`.
- **Steps done on screen without a word.** Watch for a layer's eye switched off and on, a layer dragged up or down the stack, effects enabled one at a time, a property changed in a dialog. A move of the editor's default view with a camera tool (dolly, orbit) changes the picture too: if the result is seen through it, it is a step, with the camera layer that would stand in for it. If the result depends on it, it is a step.
- **The finished example made a different way.** Sometimes the author demonstrates one route (keyframes) while the finished example on screen is driven another way (an expression, a preset). Say which is which and what is not shown.
- **Eight or more words in a row from the narration,** or the example's own on-screen sentence, or a name from the creator's project or products.
- **"Longest on" guessed.** Time it: seconds on screen for each part, from the frame numbers.
- **The finished example is a different file.** The clip at the end of a technique is often a pre-made finished project, not the build you just watched: more layers, other values, a camera move, a grade. Say plainly which the result shows, and give both sets of values when both are on screen.
- **No sizes.** Builders working from the first video's recipes had to guess every size (a ring's diameter, how far a pivot sits from its layer), because tutorials rarely say them. Measure the main elements on the result frame as shares of the picture and give them in `layout`. Calibrate each frame on the comp's own visible edges (a selected full-frame layer's handles, the picture's borders), never on the viewer's zoom percentage (the recording itself zooms, so one checker's sizes came out 15 % small) or on the viewer's guide lines (another's came out 15 % large).
- **Sizes that only hold in a 16:9 frame.** Proofs are built at 9:16, so a share of a 16:9 picture's width does not carry: an orb given as 37 % of the width came out a small ball in the tall frame, where the shot is one orb filling the frame's short side. Give each part against its own object (a corner radius against the card's height or width, a stroke and a glow's reach against the button's width, a curve's run and lean against the button and as an angle), and the main object against the frame's short side (its height in a 16:9 comp) as well as the width. An effect value in pixels (Glow Radius, a blur, a displacement size) is given with the object size it was set for (Glow radii 50, 100 and 150 on a 1206 px button), so a builder can scale it; one build at the shown radii on a smaller button got a halo four times too wide.

## What failed when the first video's recipes were built (avoid these)

All 25 recipes passed their check against the video. Building them in After Effects then showed what a check against the video cannot. `../LESSONS.md` has each case.

- **The recipe rebuilt the step, not the result.** One tip styled a single disc; the result frame shows five styled discs stacked with soft shadows between them. The build followed the steps and looked nothing like the result. Before you finish, go over the result frame and list every visible trait that makes it read the way it does (every object that carries the effect, shadows, overlaps, the background's own shading, grain). Each trait must be produced by a step, or named in `layout` with its measurements and where it comes from: another tip in this video (give that recipe's id and the figures needed here, so this recipe stands alone), or the finished example alone. "That belongs to the next tip" is not a reason to leave it out of the picture.
- **Does it apply to one object or all of them?** If the author styles one layer while the result shows several styled alike, say which, from the frame. Do not leave it in `unknowns` when the picture answers it.
- **A shown setting that does not give the shown result.** A light's Shadow Darkness read 45 % in the panel; the result frame's shadow measured more than twice as dark. When a result can be measured (a shadow's darkness against the floor, a blur's length, a glow's reach), measure it and put it in `layout`. If the shown setting cannot plausibly give it, say so.
- **Values that only mean something in the tutorial's own scene.** Fog depths, a camera's aperture, a Curves point, a font size in pixels and a blur's centre depend on the scene's scale, depths and artwork. Keep the shown value, and give the effect it has in the result in terms a builder can aim for in any scene ("each trunk keeps about half its contrast", "letters 3.2 % of the height").
- **Direction and motion written from the words, not the picture.** "Top-to-bottom shading" was diagonal; a ring's roll and its direction of travel were not given; a block of text was said to drift when it holds and then drops. For anything that moves, cut three or four frames across the result and write the direction, the speed as a share of the picture per second, and what holds still and when. For a reveal or wipe, name the corner or side the change reaches first and last, and check it against a frame at about half way. For anything turned in depth, say which edge is nearer the camera from a measurement (the same element taller at one end, an edge's slope), never "its right side towards the camera", which reads both ways. A value read while the author drags (a scrubbed number, an orbit) is a value passed through, not a key; the viewer redraws every few frames and lags the readout by 3 to 5 frames, so measure the picture only where the readout has held still for several frames, and say when the move is never played.
- **An effect credited to the wrong object.** Shadows beside a word were read as the letters' own; they came from bars standing next to it. Trace each shadow, glow or streak back to the object it starts at before you write who casts it.
- **Two figures that disagree.** A step's pixel size and the `layout` share described the same letters at two different sizes. Check each size in the steps against the result frame; when they differ, say why (the example was resized, the comp is a different size).
- **Colour from a screen recording.** A recording made on a wide-gamut display shows mid-tone blues and greens more saturated than the render is (one tutorial's blurred navy edge read blue minus red 46-51, which no mix of its navy and pale ground can give; reading its Display-P3 values as sRGB reproduced it exactly). Pure colours and near-greys come through almost unchanged. Give saturated mid-tone colours as targets only with that warning, and prefer measures that do not depend on hue (a band's width at half height, a lightness level).
- **A layer's value, not its rendered colour.** Give a solid's or stroke's colour as the value typed in its dialog, from the frame where that dialog or swatch is shown, never as the colour it renders after the effects above it: one recipe gave a ground as its after-Glow colour, a builder typed that in, and the Glow (which acted only on a blue channel of 255) did nothing. Give the rendered colour as a separate measured target.
- **Speed from a screen recording.** A recording's preview may play faster or slower than real time, and the rate changes from one playback to the next (one played 3.3 to 3.7 times real time; others in the same video ran at about real time). Never time motion against the video's own seconds: take speed from the expression (`time*40`), the key spacing, or the comp's time display across two frames.
- **Motion blur left as a switch.** Three recipes said only that motion blur was on. The tutorial's comp used a long shutter, and builds at the default 180° got about a third of its smear. When motion blur is on, measure the smear at peak speed (how far it runs past the object's own edge, or the 10–90 % ramp of a leading edge) and the speed in pixels a frame from the key spacing, and put both in `layout`. Infer the shutter angle from them (smear ≈ speed × angle ÷ 360) and mark it inferred. If the shutter needed is past 720°, say so: the timing is off, not the shutter.
- **A curved path read from its handles, not from what plays.** One cursor route was given as a handle read mid-drag and as the drawn motion path. The played route bowed about a third as much, and the first build bowed 19 % against the reference's 7 %. For a curved motion path, the target is the route the object plays: track its centre on at least six preview frames along the move and give those points in `layout`, with the handles inferred from them. Track by the object's outline (its edges against the ground, on both sides), not by its darkest or lightest pixels: a translucent object over a shaded ground pulled a lightness-only track 17–34 px off.
- **A command named from the words, not from what it does.** "Set Focus Distance to Layer" was named where the screen showed a linked focus (value red, an expression, changing with the camera), which is the menu's "Link" command. When a menu is not on screen, name the command from its visible effect and mark it `inferred`.
- **An S-curve ease written as zero speed.** An ease shown as a cubic-bezier curve (0.88, 0.14, 0.12, 0.86) is not `KeyframeEase(0, 88)`: its end slopes (0.14 ÷ 0.88 here) keep about 16 % of the average speed at each key. Give the speed as slope × the property's average speed over the segment and the influence as the handle's x share, mark both inferred, and check them against played frames.
- **A snapping move written as Easy Ease.** One recipe gave Easy Ease (influence 33.3 %) for strokes that crept, snapped and settled; the build glided, and the review caught it. Easy Ease's fastest frame is only about 1.5 times the move's average. When the played frames step harder, measure the share of the move in each frame across it, and give influences that reach that, marked inferred. After Effects' two-key ease (speed 0 at both keys, the same influence on both sides) peaks at about 1.5 × the average at 33 %, 2 × at 50 %, 3 × at 70 %, 4–5 × at 80 % and 5–8 × at 90–95 %. With an odd number of frames between the keys the fastest moment falls on one frame (17 frames at 90 %: 40 % of the move in one frame); with an even number it splits across two (18 frames at 90 %: 28 % and 28 %). Put the measured shares and the frame count in `layout` or `timing and easing`, so the builder can check the render against them.
- **Names for scripting.** Give an effect's match name only when you are sure of it; a wrong one costs a builder a round.
- **The author's code, paraphrased.** Three recipes said their bounce was "exactly the description's" expression and gave only its values; the build scripts are written from the recipe alone, never from `extras.md`, and one of them rebuilt the expression without a factor. When a step runs on an expression or script text the author gave (in `STUDY/extras.md` or readable on screen), quote the whole text word for word in a fenced block under that step, and mark it `said (description)`, `said (pinned comment)` or `shown fNNNNNN`. It is the author's shared code and may ship as given; our own words rule covers the recipe's prose, not this block.

## Output

One file per technique: `STUDY/recipes/<n>-<slug>.md`, exactly in this form.

```
## <Name, in our own words>
id: <n>-<slug>
status: unproven
source: <video id> <start>–<end> s
jobs: <one or more of: lines and shapes; layout and repetition; depth and finish; shadows and light; energy and impact; camera and 3D; type; texture; glow and colour; transitions; trending formats; B-roll scenes; workflow>
what it does for a beat: <one or two sentences: when a designer would reach for this>
what it looks like: <one to three sentences on the finished look in the tutorial's own example, described generically>
ingredients:
- <effect or feature> — <stock | paid | built-in>; <menu path if said or shown>
steps:
1. <what to do> (said | shown fNNNNNN | inferred)
   - <Property>: <value> (said | shown fNNNNNN | inferred)
layout: <sizes and positions of the main elements as shares of the picture, measured on the result frame: "ring diameter about 37% of the picture height, centred"; mark each `measured`. Then everything else the result frame shows that makes the look: which objects carry the effect, each shadow's direction, reach and darkness, overlaps and stacking order, the background's own shading, and for motion the direction and speed. A trait no step produces is named here with its source>
timing and easing: <keyframes, durations, eases given; or "none given">
variations: <what the author says can be changed; or "none given">
pitfalls: <warnings the author gives; or "none given">
longest on: <what the author spends longest on or stresses>
build notes: <how to do this by script in After Effects: effect and property names as After Effects shows them, anything that can only be done by hand in the interface>
result: start=<s> end=<s> <still | animated> crop=<x,y,w,h> frame=fNNNNNN
before: <frame showing the starting state, if any>
unknowns: <values or steps the video does not give>
```

Finish with a short report: for each technique, one line on how sure you are and what you could not read.
